The bend

Named for a great-grandfather and a curve in the road

Beauchamp Bend sits on twenty acres where the road bends — a piece of ground the Beauchamp family has worked for four generations. We farm it the way the land asks to be farmed: without a plow, with animals moving across it, and with the soil treated as the crop we're actually growing.

The walnuts are no-till. The goats rotate through the orchard and eat down the ground cover instead of us mowing it. The hens move to fresh grass every day, which is why the yolks are the colour they are. None of that is a marketing angle — it's just what works here, and it happens to make better food.

— The Dillard family

How we farm

Three things we don't compromise on

01

No-till orchard

We don't turn the soil. Every pass of a plow burns organic matter and breaks the fungal networks that feed a walnut tree. Leaving it alone builds structure, holds water, and puts more nutrients into the nut.

02

Adaptive grazing

The goats move to a new paddock on a schedule set by how fast the grass is actually growing — not a calendar. They knock back the ground cover, drop manure where it's needed, and we never run a mower through the orchard.

03

Hens on fresh grass

The laying flock gets new ground every single day. Constant access to fresh pasture means real forage in the diet, less feed bought in, and eggs that taste like something.

“Healthy soil grows healthy plants, which grow healthy animals, which grow healthy people.”
