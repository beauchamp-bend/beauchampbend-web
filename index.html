The name

Named after a great-grandfather and a curve in the road

The farm is named after my great-grandfather Beauchamp, and after the bend in the road it sits on. My family has worked this ground for four generations.

We stopped plowing it. The goats do the mowing. The hens get moved every day. That's most of it.

None of this was a philosophy when we started. The dirt was washing off the orchard and it was going to cost us. Fixing that fixed a lot of other things at the same time.

Joe Dillard

How we farm

Three things we changed

01

We stopped plowing

Plowing burns off organic matter and breaks up the soil structure that holds water through summer. Leaving it alone keeps more water in the ground and more nutrients in the nut.

02

Goats mow the orchard

The goats move to a new paddock when the grass tells us to move them, not on a calendar. They eat down the cover and leave the manure behind. We don't run a mower in the orchard.

03

Hens move every day

The laying flock gets moved to new ground daily. They eat grass and bugs instead of just feed, which cuts what we buy and makes a better egg.

“If we get the soil right, most of the rest of it works.”

Joe Dillard
